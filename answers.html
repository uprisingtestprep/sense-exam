Practice Test 1 Answer Key

  3. The correct answer is B (About 80 volts, the upper limit commonly applied to manual arc welding power sources.): Open circuit voltage on manual machines is typically 80 volts or less. That is far below the supply voltage, yet a sweating welder can still receive a serious shock from it.
  4. The correct answer is D (At least 30 minutes after the hot work ends, which is the common fire code practice.): Sparks can lodge in cracks and smolder before flames appear, so a post work watch of at least 30 minutes is standard. Five minutes is far too short for hidden ignition.
  5. The correct answer is B (Dry, heavy leather gauntlet gloves with a lining that cover the wrist and lower forearm.): Leather gauntlets resist spatter and heat and insulate when kept dry. Latex and nylon melt or burn, and thin cotton offers little protection against hot slag or electrode stubs.
  6. Exceptions exist only through a written employer procedure that verifies the person is not in danger.
  7. Readings well above normal air create a fire hazard rather than a safety margin.
  8. The correct answer is C (35 feet (11 m) in every direction, including below, unless shielded with fire resistant covers.): Fire codes and Z49.1 guidance use a 35 foot radius because sparks and spatter can bounce or fall far from the arc. Anything that cannot be moved must be shielded.
  9. The correct answer is D (A multipurpose dry chemical extinguisher rated ABC, kept within easy reach of the work area.): ABC dry chemical units handle ordinary combustibles, flammable liquids, and electrical fires. Class D and class K units are specialized, and carbon dioxide is not rated for class A fires.
  10. Ordinary eyeglasses lack side protection and impact rating.
  11. Ventilation or removing the coating at the joint prevents it, whereas flash affects only the eyes.
  12. The correct answer is D (Shade 10, within the range recommended for stick electrodes up to 5/32 inch.): Common charts place small and medium stick electrodes near shade 10. Lighter shades leave the eyes exposed to an arc brighter than they can tolerate, causing flash burns.
  13. The correct answer is C (Move to a darker lens near shade 12, because higher current produces a brighter arc.): Arc brightness rises with current and electrode size, so recommended shade numbers rise too. Shade 12 is typical for 3/16 to 1/4 inch stick electrodes.
  14. The correct answer is C (Shade 5 or 6 goggle lenses, darker than the filters used for brazing.): Gas welding calls for filters in the shade 4 to 6 range depending on thickness, with thicker work taking the darker end. Arc welding shades are far too dark for a torch flame.
  15. The correct answer is B (Place the extractor hood near the arc so the plume is captured at the source.): Local exhaust placed near the arc removes fume before it spreads, and keeping the head out of the plume reduces exposure further. A helmet does not stop fume.
  16. The correct answer is B (Shut off the machine, then switch to dry gloves and a dry insulating surface.): Wet gloves and a conductive wet floor greatly lower body resistance and provide a path for current. Dry insulation between the welder and the work is the basic control.
  17. The correct answer is B (Clamp the work lead directly to the workpiece near the weld.): Welding current takes any available path back to the machine, and chains, cranes, and cables can be damaged by arcing. Clamping close to the weld keeps the circuit controlled.
  18. The correct answer is C (Tag the cable out of service and have it repaired or replaced before any further use.): Exposed conductors can shock the welder or arc to nearby metal. Damaged leads must be removed from service and properly repaired, because tape and extra gloves do not restore insulation rating.
  19. The correct answer is C (Clean and purge the drum, test the atmosphere, and obtain a hot work permit before welding.): Residual gasoline and vapor can explode when heated, even in an empty container. Cleaning, purging, testing, and permit approval are required, and a closed drum can rupture violently.
  20. The correct answer is D (Notify workers, shut down, isolate, lock and tag, release stored energy, verify zero energy.): The standard sequence prepares workers, stops the machine, isolates and locks the source, relieves residual energy, and then confirms the machine cannot start. Testing before isolation exposes workers to danger.
  21. The correct answer is C (Flame resistant jacket buttoned to the neck, cuffless pants over boots, covered pockets.): Synthetics melt onto skin, and cuffs and open pockets catch spatter. Flame resistant natural fiber clothing with covered openings gives the best protection in out of position work.
  22. The correct answer is A (20 feet apart, or a noncombustible barrier 5 feet high with a half hour fire rating.): Oxygen feeds fires, so it is kept away from fuel gases by distance or by a fire rated barrier. Open floor of only a few feet does not meet the requirement.
  23. The correct answer is C (Leave the protective valve cap on and chain the cylinder upright to the cart.): The cap shields the valve from impact, and securing the cylinder upright prevents tipping. Lifting by the valve or rolling can damage it and release high pressure gas.
  24. The correct answer is C (Cover the openings with fire resistant material and station a fire watch below.): Sparks can travel and fall through openings and ignite materials out of the welder's sight. Sealing the gaps and watching the exposed area addresses the actual path of ignition.
  25. The correct answer is A (Rest in a dark room, apply cool damp cloths, and seek care if pain persists.): Arc eye is a surface burn that usually heals in a day or two with rest and cool compresses. Rubbing worsens corneal damage, and bright light increases discomfort.
  26. The correct answer is A (Welding screens rated to block ultraviolet and visible arc light around the station.): Purpose made screens block ultraviolet and visible light and resist burning. Ordinary tarps can ignite, and warnings or sunglasses do not prevent flash burns.
  27. The correct answer is B (Move to fresh air and let the clothing ventilate well away from any sparks or flames.): Oxygen saturated clothing can ignite violently from a single spark. Ventilation in fresh air away from ignition sources lets the fabric return to normal.
  28. The correct answer is C (Yes, because exposures at or above 85 decibels over an eight hour average require controls.): Hearing damage builds up without pain. At 85 decibels as an eight hour average, employers must provide protection and a conservation program.
  29. The correct answer is C (Hung on an insulated hook, off the table and away from grounded metal.): A live holder resting on grounded metal can arc, burn, or start a fire when someone leans on the table. An insulated hook keeps it isolated until the next weld.
  30. The correct answer is B (Reposition the head out of the rising plume and use local exhaust at the joint.): Fume rises in a hot plume, so keeping the breathing zone out of it matters most. Ventilation carries away what remains, but a visor or faster welding does not remove exposure.
  31. The correct answer is A (A face shield worn over safety glasses to guard against sparks and wheel fragments.): Grinding throws particles at high speed, so impact rated eye protection plus a full face shield is needed. A dark helmet lens is for arc light and gives poor impact coverage during grinding.
  32. The correct answer is C (A trained attendant stays outside with a way to communicate and remove the welder quickly.): Confined space entry requires an outside attendant because conditions can change and a welder can be overcome quickly. Opening size does not remove that need.
  33. The correct answer is A (Use a voltage reducing device and insulate the welder with a dry mat and gloves.): Sweat and a conductive tank wall reduce body resistance, so cutting open circuit voltage and keeping the body dry and insulated attacks the hazard directly. The other changes leave the shock path in place.
  34. The correct answer is D (Current causes harm, and wet skin lets dangerous current pass through the chest.): Body resistance falls sharply when skin is wet, so even 80 volts can drive tens of milliamps through the heart. Current path and magnitude determine severity, not voltage alone.
  35. The correct answer is D (Stop, reposition so sensors see the arc, and check the battery or use a fixed lens.): Repeated flashes damage the eyes even when brief, so the sensor view must be restored or the fixed lens used. Blocking sensors or relying on blinking offers no reliable protection.
  36. The correct answer is A (Bay B needs its own inspection and permit, because permits cover a specific location.): Each area has its own combustibles, openings, and vapors, so conditions must be checked and approved separately. A permit is tied to the specific place, work, and time period.
  37. The correct answer is D (Conducted heat can ignite the hidden foam, so check the back and station a fire watch.): Steel conducts heat through to the far side and can ignite combustibles that the welder cannot see. Hidden combustibles are checked and watched, not assumed absent.
  38. The correct answer is D (Each worker adds a personal lock to a group hasp, and power stays off until removed.): Each worker needs personal control over the energy source, so a group hasp holds separate locks. Tags alone or one person's lock leave others unprotected.
  39. The correct answer is B (Bleed the accumulator and block raised parts, since stored energy counts too.): A dead control circuit does not prove that stored hydraulic, gravity, or spring energy is gone. All forms of energy must be released or blocked before work begins.
  40. The correct answer is B (The stainless welder, since chromium and nickel in the fume raise cancer risk.): Stainless steel fume can contain hexavalent chromium and nickel, which are known health hazards. Mild steel fume still needs control, but the composition makes stainless the greater concern.
  41. The correct answer is C (A tight fitting mask cannot seal over facial hair, so use a loose fitting powered helmet.): Facial hair breaks the seal of tight fitting respirators, allowing contaminated air to leak in. Loose fitting powered units or a clean shaven face with fit testing solve this.
  42. The correct answer is D (Ventilate and find the leak, because enriched oxygen makes materials burn fiercely.): Atmospheres above about 23.5 percent oxygen are enriched and greatly increase the chance and speed of fire. The leak must be fixed and the space ventilated before any hot work.
  43. The correct answer is D (Shut the valve if reachable, evacuate, call responders, and keep the cylinder cooled from afar.): A warm acetylene cylinder can decompose and explode. Closing the valve if safe, evacuating, calling for help, and cooling from a distance limits harm.
  44. The correct answer is B (Hot work stays off until the vapor source is shut down and the area is tested.): Flammable vapors can ignite from sparks far from the arc, so the source must be controlled and the air tested. A watch cannot stop an explosion once vapors ignite.
  45. The correct answer is B (Arc ultraviolet light turns chlorinated vapor into phosgene, so vapors must clear first.): Ultraviolet radiation breaks down chlorinated vapors into toxic phosgene and hydrogen chloride. The part must dry and the vapors clear before any arc is struck.
  46. The correct answer is B (Leather sleeves or cape, welding cap, ear plugs, and a buttoned collar against spatter.): Overhead work drops spatter onto the head, neck, and shoulders and can fall into ears and collars. Leather coverage and closed openings prevent burns.
  47. The correct answer is C (Remove it from service until grounded, since a fault could energize the case.): Grounding gives fault current a safe path and trips protective devices. Without it, an internal fault can make the case live and shock the next person who touches it.
  48. The correct answer is C (No, the watch needs extinguisher training and must cover all exposed areas without other duties.): The watch looks for sparks and smoldering fires in every place the work could reach, and needs training to act fast. Dividing attention defeats the purpose.
  49. The correct answer is A (Plug the grinder into a ground fault interrupter outlet and keep cords off the wet floor.): A ground fault interrupter cuts power in a fraction of a second when current leaks, which is critical on damp floors. Longer cords and boots alone do not stop a fault.
  50. The correct answer is C (Local exhaust removes fume at its source, so engineering controls rank ahead of respirators.): The hierarchy favors removing or capturing the hazard before relying on personal protective equipment. Respirators depend on fit and use, so they come after engineering controls.
  51. The correct answer is D (Exposure controls and personal protection, which lists limits and respiratory equipment recommendations): Section 8 of the 16-section safety data sheet format holds exposure limits, engineering controls, and protective equipment guidance, including respirator selection. The reactivity and composition sections describe chemistry, not how to protect the worker.
  52. The correct answer is B (Metal fume fever caused by inhaling zinc oxide fume released from the heated coating): Zinc oxide fume from galvanized coatings causes metal fume fever, a short flu-like illness that usually clears within one to two days. Chromium poisoning would not resolve this quickly, and the symptom pattern does not fit carbon monoxide or heat illness.
  53. The correct answer is C (Chronic beryllium disease, an immune-driven lung scarring that may appear years later): Beryllium sensitizes the immune system and causes chronic beryllium disease, which can develop long after the exposure ended. Siderosis comes from iron oxide and is considered benign, and manganism and metal fume fever come from other metals.
  54. The correct answer is D (The central nervous system, producing symptoms that resemble Parkinson's disease): Manganese fume is a neurotoxin, and chronic overexposure produces tremor, gait disturbance, and mood changes similar to Parkinson's disease. Kidney damage is the hallmark of cadmium instead.
  55. The correct answer is B (Kidneys and lungs, with a possibility of cancer after prolonged exposure): Cadmium accumulates in the kidneys and damages the lungs, and it is also classed as a carcinogen. That is why plated fasteners get their own standard and exposure monitoring.
  56. The correct answer is B (The Level 1 safety exam requires a perfect score before the module is credited): SENSE Level 1 safety is the exception to the 75 percent rule and must be passed at 100 percent. Students may study and retest, because a single missed safety question can mean a missed hazard in the shop.
  57. This shop is already above the action level, so testing and protector availability are required.
  58. The correct answer is C (The valve protection cap, screwed fully onto the cylinder neck): The protection cap shields the valve from impact. A broken valve can turn a high-pressure cylinder into a projectile. A flashback arrestor belongs on fuel gas equipment during use, not on a cylinder in transit.
  59. Waiting for the weld to cool or for sparks to stop falling is not the standard.
  60. The correct answer is A (15 psig, the upper limit for free acetylene gas in use): Acetylene must not be used above 15 psig, since higher pressure risks explosive decomposition. A weak cut should be fixed by checking the tip size and oxygen setting, not by raising the fuel gas pressure.
  61. A hood placed far away captures little, and a dust mask is not designed for welding fume.
  62. Extra gas flow disturbs weld quality and does not remove fume.
  63. The correct answer is D (Oxygen content, because the combustible sensor needs adequate oxygen to read accurately): The usual order is oxygen, then combustible gases, then toxic gases. Many combustible sensors give false low readings in oxygen-deficient air, so oxygen must be confirmed first.
  64. Entering to help is how a second person becomes a second victim, so attendants never take on tasks that pull them away from the post.
  65. Dizziness and a flame that will not burn point to low oxygen, which calls for atmospheric testing and ventilation.
  66.   68.   69.   70. The signal word is about severity, not storage location or which agency regulates the product.
  71. Because a coworker will use it later, the bottle needs a label.
  72. A helmet gives little noise reduction, and waiting for hearing loss defeats the purpose.

  74.   76. The correct answer is C (Shut off the machine or use a dry insulating object to separate him from the lead): A rescuer who touches a victim still in the circuit can be shocked too.   77.   78.   79.   81. Practice Test 2 Answer Key

  1. The correct answer is B (The leg size of the fillet, measured along each plate surface, is 1/4 inch.): A number to the left of a fillet symbol states the leg size. Throat is a derived figure, about 0.707 times the leg on an equal leg fillet, so it is not what that number gives.
  2. The correct answer is A (A 1/4 inch leg fillet on each side of the stem, giving two welds in total.): Symbols on both sides of the reference line mean the weld is made on both sides of the joint. Equal sizes of 1/4 inch apply to each side separately, so two fillets are deposited.
  3. The correct answer is A (The fillet weld is continued all the way around the joint without a break.): The weld-all-around circle sits at the junction of arrow and reference line. It calls for the weld to continue around the full joint, rather than stopping at the first edge or corner.
  4. The correct answer is C (The weld is made at the erection site, not in the shop.): A flag at the arrow and reference line junction is the field weld symbol. It tells the shop not to make this weld during fabrication, and the crew welds it on site.
  5. The correct answer is A (Each segment is 3 inches long, with 8 inches center to center.): The first number at the right of the symbol is the segment length and the second is the pitch, measured center to center. Reading them in reverse is a common error.
  6. The correct answer is A (20 inches of weld, made as five separate 4 inch segments along the joint.): Segments start at 0, 12, 24, 36 and 48 inches, and the last one ends at 52. That is five segments of 4 inches, which totals 20 inches.
  7. The correct answer is C (It gives the depth of groove preparation on that side of the joint, 1/2 inch.): For groove welds, the dimension to the left of the symbol is the depth of preparation. Root opening is shown inside the symbol, and the effective throat appears in parentheses if it differs.
  8. The correct answer is C (The edges are beveled from both faces, and welding is done from each face.): V-groove symbols on both sides describe a double-V joint. Preparing and welding both faces balances heat input and reduces the weld metal needed on thick plate.
  9. The correct answer is B (Only one of the two members has its edge beveled, while the other stays square.): A bevel groove symbol has one straight leg and one angled leg, showing that only one member is beveled. A V-groove uses two angled legs for both members.
  10. The correct answer is C (A square groove, where the plate edges are not beveled before welding.): Two parallel vertical lines form the square groove symbol, which means the edges are prepared straight with no bevel. The root gap is shown inside the symbol if one is needed.
  11. The correct answer is C (The diameter of the hole in the top member that gets filled with weld.): A plug weld symbol is a rectangle, and the number at its left is the hole diameter. Pitch is shown separately at the right of the symbol, if holes repeat along the joint.
  12. The correct answer is B (Welding procedure specification number 14, listing the process and parameters.): The tail holds supplementary information such as a process, specification or procedure reference. Here it refers the welder to the procedure document that controls the weld.
  13. The correct answer is B (The far side weld is undersized and needs 5/16 inch legs.): Each side has its own size, and the number applies only to the side where its symbol sits. A sound weld that is smaller than specified is still a nonconformance.
